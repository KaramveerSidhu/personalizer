# Structural cleanup

Adapted from the structural approach in blader/humanizer v3.0.0 at commit `9862685f575c65a8247f90369951df1b3416e3d6`; see [third-party notices](../THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md). The guidance below is a condensed adaptation, not a copied pattern catalog.

Look for choices that announce importance or follow a template instead of communicating the subject. Inspect the whole paragraph before swapping words. A phrase can be perfectly appropriate: evidence, purpose, and the author's real habits matter more than any watchlist.

## What to inspect

| Family | Signs worth inspecting | Editing decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Staging | Unprompted “not X but Y” framing, rhetorical objections, grand aphorisms, fake candid openers, repeated dramatic endings | State the actual point. Keep contrasts whose two sides carry information and objections the audience really needs answered. |
| Mechanical rhythm | Forced three-part structures, identical openings, uniform sentence lengths, repeated fragments, dashes used for every relation | Rebuild the paragraph around its information. Preserve all distinct items, rankings, timing, and comparisons. A real list of three stays three. |
| Inflated claims | Sweeping importance, promotional adjectives, abstract praise, participial clauses that imply an unsupported consequence | Keep the supported claim and remove empty amplification. Do not turn association into causation. |
| Borrowed authority | Unnamed experts, vague associations, claims of acclaim without evidence | Preserve attribution and its uncertainty; clarify only using supplied evidence. Do not invent a source or upgrade an attributed claim into a fact. |
| Indirect wording | Ornate substitutes for “is” or “has,” stacked hedges, passive wording that hides a known actor, fashionable filler | Use the most precise phrasing. Keep necessary technical terms, genuine uncertainty, and passive voice when the actor is unknown or irrelevant. |
| Decoration | Bold labels on every bullet, unnecessary headings, repeated title/opening, mechanical transitions, routine closing summaries | Keep structure that helps the reader or is requested. Preserve meaningful emphasis, required sections, letter salutations and sign-offs. |
| Draft residue | Assistant praise/offers, generic “here is” preambles, knowledge-cutoff boilerplate, commentary about an earlier draft | Remove wrappers from the deliverable. Retain genuine task limitations and change history when the document is about change. |

Prefer editing a cluster over policing isolated words. Do not impose a blacklist, dash quota, sentence-length target, heading threshold, or universal ban on rhetorical questions. Punctuation inside protected text is immutable; elsewhere, source voice and the target format govern it. Do not reformat quotations from straight to curly marks or vice versa.

## Content comes first

Build an in-memory inventory of protected text and substantive claims. Apply any user-authorized exclusions explicitly. Preserve quotes, identifiers, link targets and file data exactly. Keep sufficient surrounding context so an unchanged number does not acquire a different meaning. A quote moved under a different speaker is a preservation failure even if its bytes match.

When a vague sentence has no supporting detail, make it clearer at the same level of specificity. Do not supply a plausible customer, number, event, opinion, or relationship. With an unsupported source assertion, retain its epistemic status or ask about a material conflict; never silently manufacture support.

Synthetic example:

> It is important to note that the pilot may reduce retries. This is not just a change; it is a transformative leap.

Becomes:

> The pilot may reduce retries.

The remaining “may” matters. Turning this into “The pilot reduces retries by 20%” would invent both certainty and a measurement.

## Final pass

Check that every substantive source claim survives, every output claim has support, and protected text is unchanged. Then read for leftover staging and repetitive shape. Some writing needs little editing. Without personal evidence, use a neutral voice; do not add manufactured candor, anecdotes, mixed feelings, or personality.
