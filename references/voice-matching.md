# Apply and diagnose voice

Read the chosen profile and relevant channel section. Missing dimensions are unknown, not prohibitions. Explicit preferences are directions; inferred traits are evidence weighted by confidence. Use high-confidence recurring traits consistently, medium-confidence traits flexibly, and low-confidence observations only when they fit. A fresh genuine sample can guide this task without being saved.

## Match the shape first

Prioritize sentence-length variation and syntax, then paragraph construction, compression, transitions, punctuation, and rhetorical habits. Match diction after those. Do not insert favorite words as a substitute for matching rhythm. Never borrow names, experiences, positions, or biographical facts from a sample to fill the new piece.

Match a distribution across the piece, not a quota in each paragraph. “Occasional semicolons” allows none in a short email; it does not require one. An author who sometimes uses an em dash or a fragment should retain that option even when a generic editor would remove it. Repetition, unusual punctuation, or lowercase headings need actual evidence or an explicit preference. Correct isolated accidental typos; do not cultivate errors to imitate a person.

If a distinctive trait conflicts with clarity, first rework the surrounding sentence. Do not erase an established habit reflexively. Content integrity still wins.

## Channel adaptation

Combine the supported base voice with evidenced channel overrides and the current brief. Email, chat/DM, social/LinkedIn, articles/editorials, reports, and marketing may share directness while differing in density and formality.

When no channel evidence exists, adapt conservatively to audience expectations and label the override unknown in diagnostics. Do not invent a permanent preference such as “never uses contractions in reports.” A casual sample does not establish the author's formal voice; explicit requests for formal prose still govern this task. Preserve factual qualifications across every channel, including short social posts.

## Audit separately

After the preservation audit, compare:

- Sentence and paragraph variation: does the output resemble the evidenced range?
- Syntax and compression: too many fragments, clauses, explanations, or transitions?
- Register and diction: appropriate audience, too formal, generic or promotional?
- Punctuation and formatting: forced quirks, excessive sections, or a genuine habit erased?
- Confidence: did a tentative observation become a rigid rule?

Use the metrics helper only when the text volume warrants it. Its approximate English-oriented counts are supporting evidence, not voice judgment. Compare like registers and similar amounts of prose. Do not use a tiny sample as a reliable target or judge a single paragraph against a long-work average.

Ordinary requests receive the final writing only. Diagnostics receive a few concrete mismatches tied to evidence, with uncertainty stated and short suggested edits when useful. Do not produce an AI score, authorship probability, or promise detector evasion. With no profile, distinguish generic writing observations from unavailable personal voice comparison.
